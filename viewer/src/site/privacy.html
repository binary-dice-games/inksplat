inksplat privacy policy

inksplat lets a person share a browser tab live and draw on it together with others. It has no server of its own, no accounts and no analytics. This page says what leaves your device and where it goes.

What inksplat does not do

  • It does not collect, store or sell personal data.
  • It has no analytics, advertising or tracking code, and sets no cookies.
  • It does not record the video or the drawings. Nothing is saved after a session ends.

What is shared, and with whom

  • The shared tab. The presenter chooses one tab. Its picture, a few frames a second, is sent directly (peer to peer, encrypted) to the people who have the link. Everything visible in that tab is visible to them.
  • Drawings, names, colours, pictures and turns. Sent directly between the people in the session, including images dropped on the board, the optional small picture (avatar) shown on a cursor, files dropped on the board (up to 5 MB each, sent to everyone in the session and kept only in the browser until the page closes), and requests to speak. A name and avatar are whatever the person chooses; they are kept in the browser only to prefill the next visit.
  • Network addresses. Peer-to-peer connections show each side's network (IP) address to the other. In a session, only the presenter and each viewer see each other's address; viewers do not see each other's.
  • Public trackers. To introduce people to each other, inksplat contacts public WebTorrent trackers. A tracker sees your network address and a hash that names the session. It does not see the link, the video or the drawings: what passes through it is encrypted with a key that only people with the link have.
  • STUN servers. To find a connection path, the browser asks public STUN servers (by default Google's and Cloudflare's), which see your network address. If the presenter configures a TURN server, traffic that cannot go directly passes through it, encrypted.
  • The web page. The viewer page is served by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, may log requests. The session link is in the part of the address after the #, which browsers never send to the host.

What the presenter shares

The presenter chooses a tab, a window or a screen in the browser's own picker, and the browser shows when it is shared. Only that is sent, and only to the people with the link. The presenter page does not read other pages or files. Its settings (name, colour, video quality, servers) are kept in your browser's local storage.

inksplat Support (the desktop app)

In a support session the person being helped installs inksplat Support. It shares their whole screen with the one person helping them, directly and encrypted, only after they press Allow, and stops when they press Stop, lock the screen or quit. It shows the helper's pointer on that screen and cannot click, type, read files or run programs. It has no analytics and sends no crash reports. It keeps only the name you give it, on your computer. On Windows and macOS it checks this project's GitHub Releases for a signed update when it starts, which GitHub sees like any download. The helper learns your IP address, as anybody you call over the internet does.

Your choices

Stop sharing at any time from the browser's sharing bar or the presenter page, or close the tab. Anyone who has the link can watch while the session runs, so share it only with people you trust; the presenter can lock the session, remove people, or make a new link.
